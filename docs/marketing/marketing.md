# Nomad Dashboard Marketing Messaging

## Product Summary

Nomad Dashboard is a native macOS menu bar app for digital nomads, remote workers, and traveling developers who want fast travel-ready system context without living inside a full system monitor.

## Positioning

Nomad Dashboard turns a MacBook into a compact travel instrument panel. It keeps the signals that matter close at hand in the menu bar: connection quality, internet reachability, power state, travel context, weather, local project time tracking, and optional travel-oriented tools such as alerts, local price-level context, nearby fuel prices, emergency hospitals, your embassy and consular line, surf conditions, visited places, and a local work diary. The most useful answers also reach beyond the menu bar: Siri and Shortcuts actions ("What's the emergency number in Nomad Dashboard") and desktop widgets for the verdict, the emergency number and consular line, and daylight.

The product should be positioned as:

- native and Mac-first
- compact, practical, and always nearby
- useful for life on the move, not just desk-bound system monitoring
- free to download and use
- provided as-is, with no guarantee or service-level commitment
- open source and directly downloadable outside the Mac App Store

## Value Pillars

### 1. Fast situational awareness

Open the panel and get an instant verdict (ready to work, workable, needs attention) with the place you are in, four vital tiles for internet, power, data allowance, and weather, and at most three heads-up items such as a nearing data cap, a travel advisory, or rain on the way. Tap a tile or heads-up item to jump to the full card. Layout controls stay out of the way until you choose Customize Layout.

### 2. Built for motion, not just desktops

The app is shaped around travel realities: changing Wi-Fi, variable power conditions, shifting locations, weather changes, and location-sensitive utilities.

### 3. Native macOS experience

Nomad Dashboard stays in the menu bar, avoids generic cross-platform UI, and feels like a focused Mac utility rather than a browser dashboard wrapped as an app.

### 4. Privacy-aware controls

Location-dependent features are opt-in where relevant, several travel modules are optional, and settings make it clear what is enabled.

### 5. Free and open

The app is free to download and use, released under Apache-2.0, and available through GitHub for people who want to inspect the project or follow development.

## Primary Audiences

### Digital nomads

People working while moving between countries, cities, apartments, hotels, and coworking spaces who need quick operational context from their Mac.

### Remote workers

People whose work depends on stable calls, dependable internet, battery awareness, and local conditions while working away from a fixed setup.

### Traveling developers

Engineers who care about connection quality, VPN state, latency, power draw, time zones, and practical travel overlays without opening multiple tools.

### Road-trippers, van-lifers, and RV travelers

People on the road in a car, camper, or RV who want weather, nearby fuel prices, emergency care and the local emergency number, battery and power, and mobile data allowance first. The Road trip & RV travel profile reorders the dashboard and its heads-up items for them, and can show the most important heads-up right in the menu bar. A daylight heads-up counts down to sunset and tells you when it is dark, so you can finish driving before nightfall; it is calculated on the Mac and works offline.

### Frequent travelers and consultants

People who spend long stretches on the road and want one lightweight place to glance at working conditions and nearby utility information.

## Top Use Cases

- Checking whether the current network is good enough for a call, upload, or coding session
- Tracking capped hotspot usage with Data Guard and getting warned before you blow through a mobile data allowance
- Seeing whether the laptop is charging normally or draining too fast
- Confirming public IP, device location, network-detected location, time zone, Wi-Fi context, and VPN state
- Reviewing current weather, wind, and the upcoming unified forecast before moving between locations
- Surfacing optional travel alerts and regional context while on the move
- Checking local holiday timing, city context, and whether local prices feel cheap, medium, or expensive before settling in
- Looking up nearby fuel prices or emergency hospitals when location-aware tools are useful
- Knowing whom to call and where to go abroad: your foreign ministry's consular emergency line and the nearest embassy or consulate of your country (or, when yours is not represented, the nearest EU or Nordic mission that can help), bundled offline and kept in Saved essentials
- Switching to the Road trip & RV profile for a weather, fuel, emergency-care, and power-first dashboard, and keeping the local emergency numbers available offline in Saved essentials
- Knowing how many days are left on a visa or in the Schengen area, and how many days you have spent in each country this year, counted locally from your own visited days
- Getting a short "Welcome to Morocco" briefing on arrival: local time, emergency numbers, your embassy, driving side, plugs, currency and any local holiday or Ramadan-style rhythm
- Checking UV and air quality in the weather card before you plan the day outside
- Tracking visited places locally on the Mac, with yearly travel paths, chronological stop logs, and country-day summaries by year and month
- Automatically seeing the current or nearest listed city, then browsing visual destination cards by country, monthly budget, and city internet estimates, with attributed Nomads.com ratings and meetup links
- Tracking awake work time locally on the Mac, then assigning it from the dashboard with a compact Allocate menu, interruption counts, and focus-adjusted day, week, and month summaries
- Optionally collecting stationary workplace visits from authorized device location, reviewing a local work diary, and comparing current and home time
- Keeping all of the above in a compact menu bar workflow instead of a full-size monitoring dashboard, with hidden background refreshes slowed to protect battery life

## Privacy And Control Notes

Marketing copy can safely say:

- Some features use device location only when enabled
- External IP location display can be turned off
- Several travel modules are optional
- Stay, Schengen and tax-day counts are computed on the Mac from locally saved visited days; they are a convenience, not tax or legal advice
- Air quality sends only a location rounded to about 11 km to Open-Meteo, and only while weather location is on; it has its own switch
- Explore Cities can follow the current city using existing dashboard context; matching happens locally and only a matched city slug is sent for details. It saves found and no-match results and waits for a different city before checking again. The feature can be turned off; coordinates and local travel history are not shared
- Destination photos use Nomads.com's supplied image service while the explorer is open; prominent source credits link directly to Nomads.com and its city pages, alongside a Join Nomads.com action. This is an independent app, not an affiliated Nomads.com product
- Local price level uses official public datasets and can stay limited to country fallback data where exact local sources are not available
- Project time tracking stays local to the Mac and can be turned off entirely
- The optional work diary uses authorized device location only, keeps entries local, and never uses IP address or VPN state to identify a venue
- Quiet connection alerts are opt-in, respect quiet hours and cooldowns, and do not include location or IP address
- Siri and Shortcuts answers and the desktop widgets are built on the Mac from what the app already knows; the widgets read a small file kept on the Mac that holds no IP address or coordinates, and nothing is sent anywhere for them. A widget is visible on screen, so it shows your place, emergency number, consular line and any stay or Schengen heads-up to anyone who can see your desktop
- Interruption tracking for project time tracking also stays local to the Mac and uses the same local ledger
- Development builds keep separate local time-tracking storage from the live app
- Anonymous analytics are privacy-conscious and configurable in-app, with some install and reach estimation events always collected

Avoid saying:

- zero analytics
- fully offline app
- no network calls
- no data collection whatsoever
